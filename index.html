A finetuned model is often one matrix away from its base

  1. BaseEach point is an embedding from a pretrained model. The classes are already in there, tangled together.
  2. Apply TA task matrix is a single linear map, fit from data, that carries base embeddings toward their finetuned positions.
  3. ResultAcross CLIP, DEiT, DINOv3, allMiniLM and RoBERTa on 15 vision and language benchmarks, a base model plus a task matrix outperformed linear probes and on some approached full finetuning.

Agents that imagine the next screen before they click

  1. ImagineFrom the current screen, a world model predicts what each possible action leads to, then what follows from that.
  2. Single-step trainingTaught to predict one step at a time, its imagined futures blur after about four steps.
  3. Multi-step objectiveTrained on whole rollouts, the same futures stay usable for about eight steps, with 35% less rollout error.

Fig. 3

Two world models for computer-use agents

Computer-use agents mostly learn by trial and error, which is slow and sometimes destructive. A world model lets an agent rehearse a plan inside its own prediction first. Two manuscripts from this work are in preparation, tentatively for ICLR 2027.

  • A latent world model that trains perception, dynamics and value in three separate phases: a LoRA-tuned vision encoder, an action-conditioned diffusion transformer, and a goal-grounded value head.
  • A text-space world model on the 7B UI-TARS-1.5 backbone that represents each screen as entity-centric, ID-referenced changes to the previous one.
  • A diagnosis: reconstruction objectives are misaligned with predictability in latent encoders. Capacity sweeps and overfit probes localized the generalization bottleneck.

Fig. 4

Language models that don’t have to write left to right

Masked diffusion models can fill in tokens in any order, so they can leave a hard word for later, once the words around it make it easy. That freedom has a price: the model must learn exponentially many fill-in-the-blank problems compared with an autoregressive model.

At Yale, in the Department of Applied Physics, I worked on cutting the training-time cost of adaptive masked diffusion models while keeping their flexible, order-agnostic decoding.

Research assistant, Yale University, January to July 2026. Extends an ICML 2025 Outstanding Paper on adaptive masked diffusion.

    Fig. 6

    Holding CubeSats in formation without fuel

    First author, five-student team. February to May 2026. Accepted poster, 2026 AAS/AIAA Astrodynamics Specialist Conference (AAS 26-1032).

    We tested a propellant-free, differential-drag method for CubeSat formation control. Auditing the relative-motion code, I found Schweighart–Sedwick coefficients that failed to recover Clohessy–Wiltshire dynamics as J2 goes to zero. Stroboscopic return maps and largest-Lyapunov estimates across 75 period–duty regimes then drove a new invariant-map controller.

    With p = 0.289 over eight paired one-day simulations, we framed the controller result as a reproducible test, not a superiority claim. Read the paper (DOI)

    Fig. 7

    Can proteins sense weak radio waves?

    Student researcher on a seven-person team. University of Colorado Boulder. July to August 2026.

    Weak radio-frequency fields (20 nT, 2–5 MHz) shift oxidative-stress responses in HT-1080 fibrosarcoma cells. To test the radical-pair hypothesis at the FAD cofactor, I modeled flavoproteins as graphs, residues as nodes and atomic contacts as edges. I owned the lab’s 4 MHz track end to end and completed the final 16.5 Hz analysis: a structural noise spectrum J(ω), a 75-setting sensitivity sweep and Laplacian eigenmodes for four proteins.

    Fig. 10

    Computing with magnetic noise

    Lead researcher, independent. July 2025 to March 2026. First place, county science fair; second place, school.

    I benchmarked spin-torque nano-oscillator arrays, skyrmion memristors and vortex magnetic tunnel junctions as physical reservoir computers across 500+ OOMMF micromagnetic simulations, sweeping Gilbert damping, anisotropy and coupling. Memory capacity and error on NARMA-10 and Mackey-Glass came with bootstrap confidence intervals and hypothesis tests over 11 simulation versions, failures documented as carefully as successes. It is becoming SpinRC-Bench, a benchmarking framework now in preparation.

    Fig. 12

    On the active rheology of cats

    Written for fun in July 2026. Not submitted anywhere.

    The Deborah number, De = λ/T, famously decides whether a cat is a liquid: if its relaxation time λ is short next to how long you watch it, T, it flows into whatever sink it sits in. But De can’t explain why a perfectly settled cat suddenly leaves.

    My paper adds what’s missing: a metabolic activity field that switches on at random, active stress that pushes against the sink, and an escape number, Fe, the active work available over the sink’s barrier. Leaving the sink becomes noisy barrier crossing with memory, which gives a closed-form law for how long a cat stays. No animal experiment is proposed or required.

    Read the paper (PDF)
